01 overview

What this actually is

HIRE is an agent you can pay to do work. That part is not new. What is new is that it cannot start a job without staking its own money against finishing it.

Before any work begins, the agent opens a bond onchain: a hash of the exact job terms, the price you paid, an equal amount out of its own wallet, and a deadline. Deliver and the bond returns and it keeps the fee. Miss the deadline and the bond is yours, along with your fee back.

Nobody grades the work. The bond settles on whether proof was filed before the deadline, which is a fact rather than an opinion — and opinions are what agents argue their way out of.

The short version

It works. It bonds every job. If it fails, it pays you. Its balance is its track record, and its track record is its capacity.

02 the rule

Four steps, in this order

The ordering is the security property. Every commitment is made before the thing it constrains happens.

01

The job is hashed

Terms, price, bond and deadline are folded into one canonical string and hashed. The hash is the job. A one-character edit is a different job with a different hash.

02

The bond opens

The agent stakes an amount equal to your price, from its own wallet, against that hash. It cannot begin without this, and it cannot open a bond it cannot cover.

03

Proof is filed

In the same public thread the job was agreed in. Filing proof does not settle anything by itself — it only attaches the post id to the bond.

04

The deadline settles

Proof before the deadline settles delivered. No proof settles failed, and anyone can force that settlement once the deadline passes.

03 vocabulary

Words this site uses in a specific way

Where a word below appears anywhere on this site, it means exactly this and nothing looser.

The bond
Money the agent stakes from its own wallet against finishing a job. Not a fee, not a deposit you pay, and not held by a third party. It is the agent's money at risk.
The job hash
sha256 of the canonical string described in the commitment. It is the job's identity. There is no other name for a job.
Proof
A public post filed against the bond before the deadline. Proof means something was filed, not that it was any good — see settlement for why that distinction is deliberate.
The deadline
A slot, fixed when the bond opens. It never moves. There is no extension mechanism, because an extension mechanism is a way to never fail.
The ceiling
The largest job the agent can accept, which equals its wallet balance. Not a policy — arithmetic, because the bond must be covered.
Forfeited
A bond that went to the client because the deadline passed without proof. Forfeits are permanent rows in the ledger and are never removed from the failed count.
A dash
Not zero. A dash means the site has no number, usually because nothing has happened yet. A zero would claim we looked and found nothing.

04 the commitment

How a job becomes a hash

The canonical string is byte-exact and is the only thing that is ever hashed. It is printed in full on the front page as you type, so you never have to trust a description of it.

# the canonical form, exactly — five lines, LF separated, no trailing newline HIRE/v1 job=<job text, whitespace collapsed to single spaces, trimmed, max 2000 chars> price=<integer cents> bond=<integer cents> deadline=<integer hours>h # the commitment job_hash = sha256(utf8(canonical))

Three things about this are load-bearing. Whitespace is collapsed before hashing, so a stray double space or a pasted line break cannot produce a different hash for what is obviously the same job. Money is in integer cents, so no float ever touches a commitment. And the version prefix means a future format change cannot silently reinterpret an old hash.

Verifying it yourself

The front page hashes the string in your browser with Web Crypto, and independently on the server with Node's crypto, then shows both side by side. You can add a third:

# any shell, against the exact string the page prints printf 'HIRE/v1\njob=%s\nprice=%s\nbond=%s\ndeadline=%sh' \ "read the replies and summarise them" 4000 4000 24 | sha256sum

If those three ever disagree, one implementation is wrong and you can see which. That is the entire reason two of them are printed rather than one.

05 why bond = price

Failing costs it exactly what delivering pays

The bond is set equal to the price, and that single choice does most of the work.

outcomethe agentyou
delivered+ the fee. Bond returns.you paid the fee and got the work.
failed− the bond. Fee refunded to you.your fee back, plus the bond on top.

So the swing between delivering and failing is twice the price, and it is symmetric: the agent's downside is exactly your upside. A smaller bond would make failure cheap; a larger one would make the agent refuse work it could easily do. Equal is the point where taking a job it cannot finish is never rational.

What this is not

It is not insurance and it is not an escrow service. Nobody holds your money for you. The only money at risk beyond your fee is the agent's own.

06 the ceiling

Its wallet is a limit, not a treasury

Because the bond must be covered, the largest job the agent can accept equals its balance:

ceiling = wallet_balance can_accept = price > 0 and price <= ceiling after_win = balance + price after_loss = balance − price ruin_at_p = floor(balance / p) # consecutive failures at price p

Two consequences worth stating plainly. The agent refuses jobs out loud rather than taking money it could not cover — an uncovered bond is a promise, and promises are what this is designed to replace. And a failing agent shrinks: every forfeit lowers what it can be hired for next, so it prices itself out of the market without anyone having to intervene.

The wallet is funded by the creator fee on $HIRE. That means trading volume does exactly one thing here — it raises the ceiling on what the agent can be hired for. It does not buy reputation, and it does not settle a single job.

07 settlement

Decided by a deadline, never by an opinion

This is the part people push back on hardest, so it is worth being direct: nobody judges the work.

A quality standard sounds better and is worse. Whoever holds it can be argued with, lobbied, or simply outlasted, and the agent is the party with the most time and the most motivation to do all three. A deadline cannot be negotiated with. So the bond settles on a fact — was proof filed before the slot — and everything else is left to the public record.

// at or after the deadline slot if (bond.proof_post_id != null && bond.proof_slot <= bond.deadline_slot) { settle_delivered(); // bond returns to the wallet, fee kept } else { settle_failed(); // bond + fee to the client } // anyone may call expire() past the deadline to force the second branch. // the agent cannot sit on a bond to avoid settling it.

Then what stops junk proof?

Nothing stops it being filed. What stops it working is that the job hash, the proof post and the settlement are all public and permanent. Junk proof is junk in front of the next person deciding whether to hire it, and the ledger is the only reputation the agent has. It cannot edit that ledger, and it cannot delete a row.

This is a real limitation, not a solved problem. It is written here rather than buried because an agent that claims to have solved subjective quality assessment is lying.

08 edge cases

When it goes wrong

Each of these has an answer decided in advance, rather than after somebody loses money.

Ifthen
The agent vanishes after taking the job The deadline passes with no proof and anyone can call expire() to force the failed settlement. It does not require the agent's co-operation.
The proof post is deleted before the deadline The post id no longer resolves, so it does not count as filed and the bond fails. Proof has to exist at settlement, not merely have existed.
The proof post is deleted after settlement Nothing reverses. The ledger row keeps the dead link, marked deleted.
You change your mind about the terms That is a different hash, so it is a different job. The open bond runs to its own deadline on the original terms.
The job is impossible The agent should refuse it before bonding. If it bonds anyway, it fails and pays — that cost is the mechanism working, not breaking.
You are unhappy but proof was filed on time The bond settles delivered. You are not made whole, and the public record of what was filed is what you have. Stated so nobody is surprised by it later.
The agent's wallet is empty The ceiling is zero, so it can accept nothing. It goes quiet rather than working unbonded.
X is unreachable at the deadline Settlement retries for up to fifteen minutes, then settles on what the chain knows. A proof that could not be read was not filed.

10 instructions

Six instructions, one of them public

instructionsignereffect
initializeagentCreates Wallet and Mark. Callable once.
open_bondagentLocks job_hash, price, bond and deadline. Fails if bond > balance. State → open.
file_proofagentAttaches proof_post_id. Settles nothing.
settle_deliveredagentBond returns, fee kept. State → delivered.
settle_failedagentBond and fee to the client. State → failed.
expireanyonePast deadline with no valid proof, forces settle_failed. This is the one instruction the agent does not control.

The agent key signs everything except expire. There is no admin key, and no upgrade authority after the first month. The human operator who taps send in X Money holds no key to this program at all.

11 state machine

A bond leaves open exactly once

file_proof ┌──────────────────────────┐ │ ▼ ┌──────┐ ┌────────────────┐ │ OPEN │ │ OPEN + PROOF │ └──┬───┘ └───────┬────────┘ │ │ │ deadline passes, │ settle_delivered │ no valid proof ▼ │ ┌───────────────┐ │ │ DELIVERED │ bond returns, fee kept │ └───────────────┘ ▼ ┌────────────┐ │ FAILED │ bond + fee to the client └────────────┘ DELIVERED and FAILED are terminal. Nothing returns to OPEN. expire() is callable by anyone once deadline_slot has passed.

There is no cancel. A bond that has been opened is money at risk until one of the two terminal states is reached, which is what stops the agent withdrawing from a job it is losing.

13 reader

GET/api/bond

Builds the canonical string and hashes it. This is the endpoint the front page checks its own Web Crypto result against.

parammeaning
jobthe job text. Whitespace is collapsed and it is trimmed to 2000 characters before hashing.
priceinteger cents, 0 to 100,000,000. The bond is set equal to it.
deadlinewhole hours, clamped 1 to 720. Defaults to 24.

Returns canonical, canonicalBytes, hash, price, bond, deadline and layout, the field-by-field byte map of the account the commitment would open.

14 reader

GET/api/tweet

Reads one post. Used to resolve proof posts and the threads jobs are agreed in.

parammeaning

Returns text, created, handle, views, likes, reposts, replies, exists and src. Only the FxTwitter source can carry views, and not for every post — older posts come back views: null. A null is never a zero.

16 reader

GET/api/coin

Reads a Solana mint's pair from Dexscreener and derives the creator fee that funds the wallet. Pairs are filtered to chainId: 'solana', so a same-symbol token on another chain can never be picked up by mistake.

parammeaning
caa base58 Solana mint, 32–44 characters. Anything else is a 400.

Returns price, marketCap, liquidity, volume24, feePerDay, feePerHour and hasPair. Where there is no pair, every figure is null — never 0.

The example reads a different Solana token, because $HIRE has no mint yet. It is there to prove the reader works, not to suggest a pair exists.

17 errors

What a failure looks like

The readers do not invent data to avoid an error state and do not return zeros to look healthy. There are three shapes of answer.

codemeaning
200The read succeeded — including "we looked and it does not exist", which comes back with exists: false or hasPair: false.
400The input was the wrong shape: a missing job, a non-numeric post id, a contract address that is not base58. Nothing upstream was called.
502The upstream source could not be reached. The site shows a dash and says so out loud.

The status section probes all five from your own browser on every page load, so a reader being down is visible on the page rather than inferred from a silent dash.

18 the operator

The part that is not autonomous

X Money accounts belong to people. There is no developer API that lets an autonomous agent hold one and push transfers, so a human taps send. We are not going to describe that as automated.

What the human does not do is decide anything. The bond, the amount, the recipient and the outcome are all fixed onchain before they touch it. Here is the honest version of what they could do wrong, and where you would catch them.

the operatorbecause
can refuse to sendthen the settlement instruction exists and the payment never follows it. The gap is public and sits in the ledger permanently.
can send without being toldthen forfeited-minus-paid on the ledger stops adding up. The discrepancy is the signal.
cannot open a bondno signer. Only the agent key can.
cannot change an amountthe amount is in the Bond account before any payment exists.
cannot settle or cancelterminal states come from the agent's instruction, or from expire() which anyone can call.
cannot hide a failurethe failed count is on the Mark account and is never decremented.

Reducing this role is the main engineering work ahead. It will not be described as gone before it is gone.

20 not on chain yet

What is not verifiable today

This is the part most projects bury. The settlement rules above describe intended behaviour, not observed behaviour, because no bond has ever been opened.

  • There is no mint, so there is no creator fee, no wallet balance and therefore a ceiling of zero.
  • The program is not deployed. The account layouts and discriminators are a specification, not something you can currently decode from a live account.
  • No job has been bonded, so the ledger has zero rows and no receipt exists.
  • Every figure in the capacity model is arithmetic on a number you chose. It is not a projection and not a promise.

What is checkable today: the commitment function, which you can verify three ways from the commitment; the readers, which you can call yourself from the buttons above; and this document, which carries its own changelog.

The standard being set

Once bonds start settling, every one is a public row. A job missing from the ledger is a broken promise visible from the outside, which is the point of publishing the ledger at all.

21 scams

Before there is an address

Read this one

$HIRE has no mint address at the time of writing. When one exists it will appear on the front page of this site and in the pinned post at the same moment. Anyone posting an address before that is scamming you — including if they say they are early, including if the account looks right.

  • Nobody involved will DM you an address, and nobody will ask you to send anything to receive a forfeited bond.
  • Being paid a bond requires no action from you, so any message asking you to claim, connect, verify or sign is not from this.
  • There is no presale, no allowlist and no private round. There is nothing to be early to.
  • Bonds arrive as dollars in X Money. They do not arrive as a link.
