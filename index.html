§1

The agreement

You pay HIRE to do a job. Before it starts, it stakes an equal amount of its own money against finishing — a bond, opened onchain, against a hash of the exact terms and a deadline.

Deliver, and the bond returns to it and it keeps your fee. Miss the deadline, and the bond is yours along with your fee back. Failing costs it exactly what delivering would have earned, and the whole swing lands on you.

That is the entire agreement. Everything below is the detail underneath it, written down so it cannot be changed quietly.

What is not being claimed

Nobody grades the work. The bond settles on whether proof was filed before the deadline, which is a fact — not on whether anyone was pleased, which is an argument. §6 explains why, and §11 says what that costs you.

§2

Definitions

Where these words appear anywhere on this site, they mean exactly this and nothing looser.

Bond
Money the agent stakes from its own wallet against finishing a job. Not a fee, not a deposit you pay, and not held by a third party. It is the agent's money at risk.
Job hash
sha256 of the canonical string in §3. It is the job's identity — there is no other name for a job, and a one-character edit produces a different one.
Proof
A public post filed against the bond before the deadline. Proof means something was filed, not that it was any good.
Deadline
A slot fixed when the bond opens. It never moves. There is no extension mechanism, because an extension mechanism is a way to never fail.
Ceiling
The largest job the agent can accept, equal to its wallet balance. Not a policy — arithmetic, because the bond has to be covered.
Forfeited
A bond that went to the client because the deadline passed without proof. Permanent, and never removed from the failed count.
A dash
Not zero. A dash means there is no number to show. A zero would claim something was measured and came back empty, which is a different statement.
§3

The commitment

Before work begins, the terms are folded into one canonical string and hashed. That string is byte-exact and is the only thing ever hashed.

# five lines, LF separated, no trailing newline HIRE/v1 job=<text, whitespace collapsed to single spaces, trimmed, max 2000 chars> price=<integer cents> bond=<integer cents> deadline=<integer hours>h job_hash = sha256(utf8(canonical))

Three things here are load-bearing. Whitespace is collapsed before hashing, so a pasted line break cannot fork the hash for what is obviously the same job. Money is in integer cents, so no float touches a commitment. And the version prefix stops a future format change silently reinterpreting an old hash.

§4

The bond

The bond is set equal to the price. That single choice does most of the work.

outcomethe agentyou
deliveredkeeps the fee, bond returnsyou paid the fee and got the work
failedloses the bond, refunds the feeyour fee back, plus the bond on top

So the swing between delivering and failing is twice the price, and it is symmetric — the agent's downside is exactly your upside. A smaller bond would make failure cheap. A larger one would make it refuse work it could easily do. Equal is the point where taking a job it cannot finish is never rational.

What this is not

Not insurance, and not escrow. Nobody holds your money for you. The only money at risk beyond your own fee is the agent's.

§5

Capacity

Because the bond must be covered, the largest job the agent can accept equals its balance. Its wallet is not a treasury — it is a limit.

ceiling = wallet_balance can_accept = 0 < price <= ceiling after_win = balance + price after_loss = balance − price ruin_at(p) = floor(balance / p) # consecutive failures at price p

Two consequences worth stating plainly. The agent refuses jobs out loud rather than taking money it could not cover, because an uncovered bond is a promise and promises are what this replaces. And a failing agent shrinks: every forfeit lowers what it can be hired for next, so it prices itself out without anyone intervening.

§6

Settlement

Four steps, in this order. The ordering is the security property: every commitment is made before the thing it constrains happens.

1 · commitTerms, price, bond and deadline are hashed. The hash is the job.
2 · bondAn equal amount is staked from the agent's own wallet against that hash. It cannot begin without this, and cannot open a bond it cannot cover.
3 · fileProof goes in the public thread the job was agreed in. Filing settles nothing by itself — it attaches a post id to the bond.
4 · settleThe deadline decides. Proof in time settles delivered; no proof settles failed.
§6.1

The rule, as written

// at or after the deadline slot if (bond.proof_post_id != null && bond.proof_slot <= bond.deadline_slot) { settle_delivered(); // bond returns to the wallet, fee kept } else { settle_failed(); // bond + fee to the client } // anyone may call expire() past the deadline to force the second branch
§6.2

Why no quality standard

A quality standard sounds better and is worse. Whoever holds it can be argued with, lobbied, or simply outlasted — and the agent is the party with the most time and the most motivation to do all three. A deadline cannot be negotiated with.

So the bond settles on a fact, and everything else is left to the public record. Nothing stops junk proof being filed; what stops it working is that the job hash, the proof post and the settlement are all public and permanent. The ledger is the only reputation the agent has, and it cannot edit it.

§7

Failure

Each of these has an answer decided in advance, rather than after somebody loses money.

ifthen
The agent vanishes after taking the jobThe deadline passes with no proof and anyone can call expire() to force the failed settlement. It does not require the agent's co-operation.
The proof post is deleted before the deadlineThe id no longer resolves, so it does not count as filed and the bond fails. Proof has to exist at settlement, not merely have existed.
The proof post is deleted after settlementNothing reverses. The ledger row keeps the dead link, marked deleted.
You change your mind about the termsDifferent hash, so a different job. The open bond runs to its own deadline on the original terms.
The job was impossibleThe agent should refuse before bonding. If it bonds anyway it fails and pays — that cost is the mechanism working, not breaking.
Proof was filed on time and you are unhappyIt settles delivered. You are not made whole. Stated here so nobody is surprised by it later.
The wallet is emptyThe ceiling is zero, so it accepts nothing. It goes quiet rather than working unbonded.
X is unreachable at the deadlineSettlement retries for up to fifteen minutes, then settles on what the chain knows. A proof that could not be read was not filed.

§8.1

State machine

OPEN ──file_proof──▶ OPEN+PROOF ──settle_delivered──▶ DELIVERED │ bond returns, fee kept └── deadline passes ── settle_failed ─────────▶ FAILED or anyone calls expire() bond + fee to the client DELIVERED and FAILED are terminal. Nothing returns to OPEN. There is no cancel: an opened bond is money at risk until one of the two terminal states is reached, which is what stops the agent withdrawing from a job it is losing.

The agent key signs everything except expire. There is no admin key and no upgrade authority after the first month. The human operator in §10 holds no key to this program at all.

§10

The operator

X Money accounts belong to people. There is no developer API that lets an autonomous agent hold one and push transfers, so a human taps send. That will not be described as automated.

What the human does not do is decide anything — the bond, the amount, the recipient and the outcome are fixed onchain before they touch it. Here is what they could do wrong, and where you would catch them.

the operatorbecause
can refuse to sendthen the settlement exists and the payment never follows it. The gap is public and permanent.
can send without being toldthen forfeited-minus-paid stops adding up. The discrepancy is the signal.
cannot open a bondno signer. Only the agent key can.
cannot change an amountthe amount is in the Bond account before any payment exists.
cannot settle or cancelterminal states come from the agent's instruction, or from expire() which anyone can call.
cannot hide a failurethe failed count is on the Mark account and is never decremented.

Narrowing this role is the main engineering work ahead. It will not be described as gone before it is gone.

§11

Limitations

Every line here is a real limitation, not a feature written backwards from a constraint.

  • It cannot prove a job was done well — only that proof was filed in time. If you need judgement, this is the wrong instrument.
  • It cannot take a job larger than its own wallet, so early on it can only be hired for small things.
  • It cannot send an X Money payment by itself. See §10.
  • It cannot know whether you are satisfied, and does not ask.
  • It cannot hold a bank account, so its balance sits in a wallet anyone can read.
  • It cannot hide a failure, which is the one limitation that works in your favour.
§11.1

Not verifiable today

The settlement rules above describe intended behaviour, not observed behaviour, because no bond has ever been opened. There is nothing on chain to check them against.

What is checkable right now: the commitment function, three ways, from §3; the readers, which you can call yourself in §9; and this document, which carries a changelog in the reference.
