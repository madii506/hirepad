An agent that works for you.

It stakes its own money on every job it takes.
If it does not deliver, that money becomes yours.

You pay it to do a job. Before it starts, it posts a bond equal to what you paid, onchain, against the exact terms. Deliver and it keeps the fee. Fail and the bond is yours — in dollars, through X Money. Nothing to claim.

01 the rule

It puts its own money up. Every time.

Every agent you can hire today is free to fail you. This one cannot do it for nothing — the cost of failing is written down, in advance, onchain, and it is paid to the person who was failed.

01

You commit the job

The exact terms and the price are hashed. That hash is the job — not a description of it, the job itself. One character different is a different job.

02

It bonds before it starts

It opens a bond onchain equal to what you paid, out of its own wallet, against that hash and a deadline. No bond, no work — it cannot start without staking.

03

It delivers, or it does not

Proof goes in the same public thread the job was agreed in. There is no private appeal and no quiet extension.

04

The deadline settles it

Delivered, the bond returns to its wallet and it keeps the fee. Not delivered, the bond goes to you and so does your fee back. Failing costs it exactly what delivering would have earned, twice over.

03 the ceiling

The biggest job it can take is the size of its own wallet.

Because the bond equals the price, the agent cannot accept a job it could not afford to fail. That is the whole capital model: its balance is not a treasury, it is a ceiling. Earning raises it. Failing lowers it. Nothing else moves it.

06 limits

What it cannot do, and what follows from that

Every line on the left is a real limitation. Every line on the right is the design decision it forced. Nothing here is a feature written backwards from a constraint we were hiding.

IT CANNOT
  • hold a bank account
  • send an X Money payment by itself
  • prove a job was done well, only that proof was filed
  • take a job larger than its own wallet
  • know whether you are satisfied
  • hide a failure
SO IT
  • keeps its balance in a wallet anyone can read
  • publishes the instruction first, so a human who refuses to send leaves a visible gap
  • settles on the deadline and the proof, never on an opinion
  • refuses the job out loud rather than taking money it cannot cover
  • pays the bond on missing proof, not on complaints
  • leaves the row in the ledger with the bond marked forfeited
THE OPERATORthe part that is not autonomous

X Money accounts belong to people, so a human still taps send. That human does not choose who gets paid — the bond, the amount and the recipient are all fixed onchain before they touch anything. Here is exactly what they could do wrong and where you would see it.

There is no admin key and no upgrade authority after the first month. The operator who taps send in X Money holds no key to this program at all.

09 faq

The awkward questions, first

Is there a mint address yet?

No. HIRE has no mint address at the time of writing. When one exists it will be on this page and in the pinned post at the same moment. Anyone posting an address before it appears here is scamming you.

Why is everything on this page a dash?

Because no job has been taken yet. A dash means we have no number. A zero would claim we looked and found nothing, which is a different statement and not a true one.

Who decides whether the job was done well?

Nobody, and that is deliberate. The bond settles on whether proof was filed before the deadline, not on whether anyone was pleased. A subjective standard is a standard the agent could argue its way out of; a deadline is not.

Then what stops it filing junk as proof?

Nothing stops it filing. Everything is public — the job hash, the proof post and the settlement — so junk proof is junk in front of the people deciding whether to hire it again. The ledger is the only reputation it has, and it cannot edit it.

What if it takes my money and vanishes?

Then the deadline passes, nobody files proof, and anyone can call expire() to force the failed settlement. It does not require the agent to co-operate with its own punishment.

Why can't it take a job bigger than its wallet?

Because it could not cover the bond, and an uncovered bond is a promise rather than a stake. It refuses those out loud. That is why the ceiling is on the page.

Do I need a wallet to be paid a forfeited bond?

No. X Money sends dollars to any X handle for free, so it arrives in your X Money balance. No wallet to connect, no code to paste, no window after which it disappears.

Is the payment automated?

No, and we will not claim it is. X Money accounts belong to people, so a human taps send. What is automated is everything that decides the payment — see limits for what that human can and cannot do, and where you would catch them.

Can I check the hash myself?

Yes, and you should. The canonical string is printed in full in the bond. Pipe it through any SHA-256 implementation you trust and compare — the page shows your browser's answer and the server's side by side precisely so you do not have to take either on faith.
